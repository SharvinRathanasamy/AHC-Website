Halal Certificate Suspension, Withdrawal & Compliance Risk
Protecting Your Premises' Halal Certification

Obtaining a Malaysian Halal Certificate is an important milestone for any food business. However, maintaining certification requires continuous compliance with the applicable Malaysian Halal Certification requirements.

A certified food premises may face non-compliance, suspension, withdrawal or termination of its Halal Certificate when significant requirements are not maintained or when issues arise that affect the halal integrity of the operation.

At AHC Resources, we help businesses identify potential risks early and strengthen their halal compliance systems to support the continued maintenance of their halal certification.

What Can Put a Premises' Halal Certificate at Risk?

Potential compliance risks may include:

1. Changes to Ingredients or Raw Materials
Introducing new ingredients, changing raw materials or using materials without appropriate supporting halal documentation may create compliance concerns.

2. Changes to Suppliers
Changing suppliers without properly reviewing and maintaining the required halal documentation can create risks within the halal supply chain.

3. Changes to Premises or Operations
Changes to the certified premises, address, production area, processes, products, brands or business operations may require notification and appropriate action.

4. Failure to Maintain Halal Documentation
Incomplete, outdated or inconsistent records may make it difficult for a business to demonstrate that its halal controls are being properly maintained.

5. Non-Compliance During Inspection or Audit
Findings identified during an official inspection or audit may require corrective action. Failure to adequately address relevant non-conformities can place the certification status at risk.

6. Failure to Maintain the Halal Management System
Businesses are expected to maintain the applicable halal management requirements and supporting records. Weak implementation or inadequate monitoring may result in compliance concerns.

7. Misuse of the Halal Certificate or Logo
The Malaysian Halal Certificate and Halal logo must be used according to the applicable requirements. Improper use, alteration or misuse may result in enforcement action.

8. Practices That Raise Shariah Concerns
Handling, processing, storage or other practices that create concerns from the perspective of Shariah may affect the status of halal certification.
